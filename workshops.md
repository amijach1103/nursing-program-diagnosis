# The workshop series

A kickoff and six virtual workshops, from 2021 into 2022, to help a nursing school's faculty and leadership act on the program analysis. Agendas and outlines are plans; where session notes show what happened, it's marked.

## The arc

| Session | Planned focus | What the documents show |
|---|---|---|
| Kickoff | A shared understanding of competency-based education and of the project plan | Notes from part 1: the benefits and models of competency-based education, the student journey and what the work needs from faculty |
| Leaders | Walk the analysis in order: the current competency map, what's missing, progression, then a plan to engage faculty | Agenda and worksheets |
| Faculty | Results of the analysis, an activity to evaluate them, and the selected faculty group's role and next steps | Agenda |
| Assessment design | Backward design: assessment first, then an assessment strategy and feedback | Agenda |
| Curriculum mapping | Two sessions a week apart: map competencies to courses, then a worked example from lesson map to course map to curriculum map | Notes from both sessions |
| Active learning strategies | Four breakout rooms covering the six characteristics of active learning in the standards, with facilitators moving between them | Notes |
| Program evaluation | Evaluation at the program and course level, and a wrap-up of the series | Outline |

## How each one was built

Most workshop plans used breakout rooms, by program, by semester or track, or by topic. The playbook is a self-assessment: yes or no statements for each element, then a task table naming who's responsible, accountable, consulted and involved. A leaders' worksheet turned the recommendations into tasks, each tied to the workshop it should be done by, an internal deadline and an owner.

## What the notes show

In curriculum mapping, faculty found the work daunting, debated whether to start at the lesson or the course level, and asked for a concrete example from start to finish and a worksheet or template. The second session's notes list time as a barrier, and office hours and one-to-one support.

In active learning strategies, the notes record faculty taking an active part and asking for a space to share strategies with each other.

## Materials

A one-page checklist walks faculty through the elements as questions about their own course, starting with assessment. For the later workshops, I packaged the recordings with worksheets and guides, and ran office hours so faculty leads could support their colleagues.

## What happened, and what we'd change

Some faculty were very worried about their lack of time, so some sessions made room for them to talk about it, and we adjusted.

The checklists became a resource sent to all faculty, and the active learning workshop was opened to the whole faculty. The feedback we took into the next engagement: shorter workshops and a stronger pre-assessment. Change in faculty practice wasn't measured.
