# The workshop series

A kickoff and six virtual workshops, from 2021 into 2022, to help a nursing school's faculty and leadership act on the program analysis. [30, 31, 33] Agendas and outlines are plans; where session notes show what happened, it's marked. [LEDGER G6]

## The arc

| Session | Planned focus | What the documents show |
|---|---|---|
| Kickoff | A shared understanding of competency-based education and of the project plan [54] | Notes from part 1: the benefits and models of competency-based education, the student journey and what the work needs from faculty [53] |
| Leaders | Walk the analysis in order: the current competency map, what's missing, progression, then a plan to engage faculty [35] | Agenda and worksheets [35, 36] |
| Faculty | Results of the analysis, an activity to evaluate them, and the selected faculty group's role and next steps [50] | Agenda [LEDGER G6] |
| Assessment design | Backward design: assessment first, then an assessment strategy and feedback [38] | Agenda [38] |
| Curriculum mapping | Two sessions a week apart: map competencies to courses, then a worked example from lesson map to course map to curriculum map [39, 41] | Notes from both sessions [39, 40, 42] |
| Active learning strategies | Four breakout rooms covering the six characteristics of active learning in the standards, with facilitators moving between them [44] | Notes [45] |
| Program evaluation | Evaluation at the program and course level, and a wrap-up of the series [52] | Outline [LEDGER G6] |

## How each one was built

Most workshop plans used breakout rooms, by program, by semester or track, or by topic. [51] The playbook is a self-assessment: yes or no statements for each element, then a task table naming who's responsible, accountable, consulted and involved. [37] A leaders' worksheet turned the recommendations into tasks, each tied to the workshop it should be done by, an internal deadline and an owner. [36]

## What the notes show

In curriculum mapping, faculty found the work daunting, debated whether to start at the lesson or the course level, and asked for a concrete example from start to finish and a worksheet or template. [40] The second session's notes list time as a barrier, and office hours and one-to-one support. [42]

In active learning strategies, the notes record faculty taking an active part and asking for a space to share strategies with each other. [45]

## Materials

A one-page checklist walks faculty through the elements as questions about their own course, starting with assessment. [46] For the later workshops, I packaged the recordings with worksheets and guides, and ran office hours so faculty leads could support their colleagues. [47]

## What happened, and what we'd change

Some faculty were very worried about their lack of time, so some sessions made room for them to talk about it, and we adjusted. [43]

The checklists became a resource sent to all faculty, and the active learning workshop was opened to the whole faculty. [48] The feedback we took into the next engagement: shorter workshops and a stronger pre-assessment. [48] Change in faculty practice wasn't measured. [48]
