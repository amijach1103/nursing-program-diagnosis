# Diagnosing a nursing program against new competency standards

A client engagement I led in 2021 and 2022 at a research and development lab [49]: a university nursing school's bachelor's and master's programs mapped against the American Association of Colleges of Nursing's new competency standards, then a workshop series to help faculty act on the results. [4, 5, 31]

**About 80** courses analyzed in depth · **10** domains, **45** competencies · **a kickoff and 6** workshops [10, 15, 30]

## Context

The American Association of Colleges of Nursing (AACN) asked nursing schools to move to a competency-based model. [1] A prominent nursing school came to us to start that process, and I led the first engagement: diagnose where the programs stood, and kick off the change with faculty and leadership. [2, 4] We talked with students, faculty and leadership to understand the student experience. [6]

## How we diagnosed the programs

**The courses, mapped.** I wrote the program analysis reports for both programs. [17] For each course, we reviewed the syllabus, schedules, sample assessments and the activities in the LMS. [12] Each objective and its assessments were mapped to the domain, competency and sub-competency of the new standards it addressed. [18] The reports analyzed about 80 courses in depth and tagged around 500 learning objectives. [10, 16] Courses taught outside the nursing school were listed but not analyzed. [11]

**Coverage, four ways.** The reports show how often each domain is addressed, which competencies in each domain show up, how they're spread across semesters or tracks, and whether the level fits the program. [20] The standards label sub-competencies as entry level or advanced, so we could check that a bachelor's program wasn't leaning on graduate-level ones. [21]

**Activities, against the standards' own definition.** We split the standards' definition of active learning into six yes/no questions and asked them of every course we analyzed in depth. [22]

**Recommendations.** Among them: match the verb in each objective to the level of competency the assessment asks for, and build in the diversity, equity and inclusion competencies the standards require. [25, 26] The report also suggested an assessment template, pre-assessments at the start of a semester, a common course home page and spaces for faculty to talk about student support. [27]

## The workshop series

I designed and ran the series with a steering committee of administrators and faculty, over seven months. [32] It was a kickoff and six workshops: one for leaders, one for faculty, then assessment design, curriculum mapping, active learning strategies and program evaluation. [30] The workshops were virtual, and I owned the schedule and the communications with faculty. [33] Most workshop plans used breakout rooms, by program, by semester or track, or by topic. [51]

Some faculty were very worried about their lack of time, so some sessions made room for them to talk about it, and we adjusted. [43]

For the later workshops, I packaged the recordings with worksheets and guides, and ran office hours so faculty leads could support their colleagues. [47]

## What happened

In the follow-up, the checklists became a resource sent to all faculty, the active learning workshop was opened to the whole faculty, and the program analysis was called a good starting point. [48] We took three things into the next engagement: a summary at the top of the report, shorter workshops and a stronger pre-assessment. [48] Change in faculty practice after the engagement wasn't measured. [48]

## Design decisions

- **Use the standards' own structure.** Results follow the standards' domains, competencies and sub-competencies. [14]
- **Say what we couldn't see.** We judged what was visible in the course materials, and the report says so: where evaluation criteria weren't clear, it allows they might be clear in class. [23]
- **The report feeds the workshops.** The analysis was meant as a baseline, with its examples used to model changes with faculty. [29] The leaders' workshop agenda walked through it in order: the current map, what's missing, progression, then a plan. [35]
