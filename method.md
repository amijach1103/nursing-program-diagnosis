# The program analysis: method and report format

How we mapped a nursing school's courses against the AACN's competency standards, and how the reports were laid out. No client data, course names or results are here, and the lab's own framework isn't reproduced.

## Inputs

- **Access to every course.** We asked for LMS access to every course in the program's schedule, and each report says how many we got and how many we didn't.
- **What we read per course.** The syllabus, schedules, sample assessments and the activities in the LMS.
- **Scope.** About 80 courses across both programs were analyzed in depth. Courses taught outside the nursing school were listed but not analyzed.

## The mapping

1. **One row per objective.** For each course, every learning objective went in a table with the assessments tied to it.
2. **Tag it to the standards.** Each objective was mapped to the domain, competency and sub-competency it addressed: 10 domains and 45 competencies. Around 500 objectives were tagged across both programs.
3. **Check the level.** The standards mark sub-competencies as entry level or advanced, so we counted both and checked them against the program's level.
4. **Check the activities.** We split the standards' definition of active learning into six yes/no questions and answered them for every course we analyzed in depth: does it ask students to act on what they know, are the objectives clear, is practice guided, is progress assessed regularly, is feedback frequent, and do students assess their own competence?
5. **Say what we couldn't see.** Where something wasn't visible in the materials, like evaluation criteria, the report says so and allows it may be clear in class.

## The report, section by section

1. **Introduction and method**, including the access table.
2. **Coverage across the program**: how often each domain is addressed, then which competencies show up in each domain. Domain counts come from the learning objectives.
3. **Coverage over time**: a map by semester for the bachelor's program and by track for the master's.
4. **Level**: entry versus advanced sub-competencies.
5. **Active learning**: the six questions, with the courses that do and don't meet each one.
6. **Recommendations**, grouped by the lab's framework and closed with a summary table.
7. **Review by course**: the objective-to-standards table and the six active learning answers for each course.

## Recommendations that applied across both programs

- Match the verb in each objective to the level of competency the assessment asks for. The report's example is an objective that says "discuss" with an assessment that asks for analyzing or demonstrating.
- Build in the diversity, equity and inclusion competencies the standards require, especially self-reflection and noticing bias.
- Make evaluation criteria visible with an assessment template, and add pre-assessments at the start of a semester.
- Give every course a common home page in the LMS, and make spaces for faculty to talk about student support.

## What we'd change

Feedback we took into the next engagement: a summary at the top of the report.
